Privacy Policy — Khao

Last updated: 2 August 2026

Khao is designed to be private. This policy explains, in plain language, exactly what the app does and does not do with information. It is deliberately short because Khao collects very little.

The short version

Khao has no account system and no analytics. Everything you do in the app — your taste preferences, diet, allergies, household, meal times, likes and saved dishes — is stored only on your device. Khao does not operate a server that receives your personal data.

The matching happens on your phone

This is worth stating plainly rather than leaving you to infer it. Working out which dish to suggest — reading your palate, your allergies, your household's rules, the time of day and the time of year, and ranking the whole recipe list against them — runs on your device, every time. There is no request to anybody's server in that path, because there is no server. It is why the app still works with no network at all, and it is why nobody, including us, has to be trusted with your allergies.

The microphone and the camera

More → Leftover Remix lets you speak what is in your fridge, or photograph it, instead of typing it. Both are optional, both are used only on that one screen, and typing works exactly as it always has. Khao uses the microphone and the camera nowhere else in the app.

Nothing is recorded and nothing is uploaded.

  • Speaking. Khao uses Apple's speech recognition with on-device recognition required. If your iPhone does not have the offline dictation files for the language you are speaking, Khao refuses to listen and tells you so — it does not fall back to sending your voice to a server. That is a setting in the code, not a preference you or we can change. No recording is created, no audio file is saved, and nothing is written to storage at any point.
  • Photographing. Khao reads the picture with Apple's Vision framework, which runs on the phone. It looks for words printed on a packet or jar, and for what the picture appears to contain. The image is not uploaded, not written to disk, and is gone as soon as you leave the screen. If you choose a photo you already have rather than taking a new one, iOS hands Khao that single image out of the system picker — Khao is never given access to your photo library.

Khao then shows you what it thought it heard or saw, and adds nothing until you tick it. The only thing either route can leave behind is the list of ingredient names you confirmed, saved to your own kitchen list on this phone, exactly as if you had typed them.

iOS will ask your permission for the microphone, for speech recognition and for the camera the first time you use one of these. If you say no, that route stops being offered, the app tells you how to turn it back on, and nothing else changes. You can change your mind at any time in Settings → Khao.

What stays on your device

The following is saved locally on your iPhone or iPad (in the app's own storage) and is never transmitted to us:

  • Your language, diet, allergen and dislike settings
  • Household members and their individual diets and allergies
  • The dishes you like, skip, or mark "never again" (how Khao learns your palate)
  • Meals you log, dishes you save for later, and your kitchen/pantry items
  • Your meal times and reminder preferences
  • Outstanding household invitations you have created or accepted, and the one-time keys that go with them (see "Inviting someone to your household" below)

You can see all of it — You → Privacy & your data → Export my data produces a readable file. That file is created only when you tap the button, it is stored inside the app's own private space on the phone, it is left out of iCloud and iTunes backups, and it is deleted again as soon as you leave that screen. You can erase everything from You → Privacy & your data → Delete my data, which also removes any export. Deleting the app removes all of it too.

Inviting someone to your household

Household → Invite by link lets you send someone a link so they can fill in their own diet, allergies and dislikes on their own phone, instead of you guessing.

Nothing about this passes through us, because there is nothing for it to pass through. There is no server, no account, and no database. Everything each phone needs travels inside the link itself.

  • The invitation you send contains only: an identifier for your household, an identifier for the slot being filled, the name you are known by in the app, the name you suggested for them, an expiry date, and a one-time key. It contains nothing about anybody's diet, allergies or beliefs.
  • The reply they send back contains what they filled in — and it is encrypted before it leaves their phone, with a key that existed only inside your invitation. Anyone who sees the reply on its own — in a group chat, in a forwarded message, in a phone backup — cannot read it, and cannot even tell which household it belongs to. It also cannot be altered: if a single character changes, your phone refuses it rather than accepting a tampered-with version of somebody's allergies.
  • We never see either one. We have no way to. The invitation is carried by whatever you choose to send it with — WhatsApp, iMessage, a QR code held across the table — and those apps' own privacy policies apply to the message.
  • If you tap an invitation without the app installed, you land on a web page offering you the App Store. The invitation sits in the part of the web address after the #, which browsers never send to a web server — so the site hosting that page cannot see it even in principle, and it is not stored, logged or forwarded anywhere.
  • Nothing is added to your household until you say so. Opening a link only shows you what arrived, in plain words; nothing changes until you tap the button.
  • Invitations expire after 30 days. Removing a household member, or cancelling an invitation, destroys the key on your phone — after which any reply to it can no longer be read at all.

Two honest limits, stated rather than glossed over. First, anyone who sees both the invitation and the reply can read the reply, because the invitation is what carries the key; there is no way around that without a server holding a secret, and a server holding other people's allergies is precisely what Khao refuses to have. Second, an invitation is a message: once sent, you cannot un-send it, in the same way you cannot un-send any other message.

How long it is kept

For as long as you keep the app, and not a moment longer. Because Khao holds nothing about you anywhere except on your own device, there is no second copy sitting on a server with a retention clock running on it. Your settings and history stay on the phone until you erase them from You → Privacy & your data → Delete my data, or until you delete the app — either one removes the lot straight away. The export file is the shortest-lived thing here: it exists only while that screen is open. We keep nothing, so there is nothing for us to hold on to, and no backup of it for you to ask us about.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Khao schedules local meal reminders on your device. These are generated on the phone; no reminder content is sent through us. Khao does not use remote push notifications.

No images loaded from the internet

Khao displays every dish as an illustration drawn on the device. It does not download photos from any server, and it contains no analytics, no advertising software and no third-party frameworks of any kind.

The one thing Khao downloads

So that new dishes can be added without waiting for an app update, Khao checks once per launch whether a newer dish list is available. It fetches a single public file over HTTPS: raw.githubusercontent.com/anirudhkapoor1996-web/khao-catalog/main/catalog.json

This request contains nothing about you — no account, no identifier, no cookie, no preferences, no search terms, no request body. It is the identical request for every user of the app, and it is the only automatic network request Khao makes. The file is a list of recipes and nothing else: Khao downloads no code, and every feature and rule in the app is the same before and after.

The downloaded list is validated on the device before it is used — dish count, unique IDs, and allergen data are all checked — and anything that fails validation is discarded. If the request fails, or you are offline, the app works exactly as before using the recipes built into it.

Like any request to any website, GitHub's servers will see the IP address it came from, in the same way they would if you loaded a web page. We have no access to those records and receive nothing from them.

Ordering out

When you tap to order a dish, Khao opens Zomato or Swiggy — either the installed app or their website — with the dish name as a search. Khao sends nothing else, and only when you tap. Anything you do there is governed by their privacy policies, not this one.

Children

Khao is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them. Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, a child is anyone under 18 years of age. Khao has no account, collects no age information and builds no profile of any user, so it does not track or profile children — or anyone else.

Your rights

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you have the right to know what personal data is held about you, to correct or erase it, to complain about how it has been handled, and to nominate another person to exercise these rights on your behalf. Because Khao holds your information only on your own device, you can exercise the first two immediately and without asking anyone, from You → Privacy & your data.

Grievances

If you are unhappy with how your information has been handled, contact Anirudh Kapoor, anirudhkapoor1996@gmail.com. We will respond within 90 days, as required by Section 13 of the Act, and usually much sooner. If you are not satisfied with the response, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Data we sell or share

None. Khao does not sell, rent, or share your personal data with third parties, for advertising or for any other purpose. Your diet, allergies, household and swipes are never used to target advertising — that is a limit built into how the app works, not a promise about what is on the screen today, and it does not expire.
